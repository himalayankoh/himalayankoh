You do not need a special recipe to use it. The useful questions are simpler. Is the salt intended for cooking? Does the grain size suit the dish? How much does the recipe need? And where should you keep the bag once it is open?

This guide starts with those decisions, so your next purchase has a clear purpose and your next meal is easier to season.

Start with the label, not the color

When shopping for kitchen salt, choose a product explicitly sold for edible or culinary use. A pink appearance alone does not tell you what a product is intended for. Livestock salt blocks, decorative pieces and cooking salt are different product categories; keep their uses separate.

Read the product description for grain size, ingredients, pack size and handling instructions. If any of those details are unclear, ask before ordering. Fine salt or coarse salt?

Grain size changes how salt fits into your cooking routine. Fine grains are convenient when you want seasoning distributed through a dish. Coarse grains are useful when you plan to grind them or deliberately leave some texture at the end.

  • For everyday mixing: consider fine culinary salt for dressings, sauces and seasoning mixtures. Add a small amount, mix thoroughly and taste when the food is ready to taste.
  • For a grinder: choose coarse culinary salt that suits the grinder manufacturer's instructions. Check compatibility before filling it; not every mill is designed for salt.
  • For a finishing touch: use a light amount on a finished dish if you enjoy the texture. A finishing sprinkle is part of the total seasoning, so leave room for it earlier in the recipe.

If you buy only one size, choose around the meals you cook most often. A bag that suits your routine is more useful than one bought simply because the crystals look attractive.

Measure carefully when changing salts

A measuring spoon measures volume, not weight. Salt crystals differ in size and density, so equal spoonfuls of different salts do not always behave as interchangeable amounts. Morton's conversion guide explains this difference, but its conversion numbers apply to Morton products and should not be treated as measurements for Himalayan Koh salt.

Follow the salt type named in your recipe when possible. If a recipe specifies grams, use that weight with a scale suitable for small quantities. If it specifies a particular brand or a spoon measure, do not assume a different grain size is an exact substitute.

For an ordinary soup or vegetable dish, make small adjustments after mixing. For baking or another recipe where the amount is fixed, follow the recipe's measurement instructions instead of improvising.

Three easy places to use it

A tray of roasted vegetables

Put your prepared vegetables in a bowl, add the oil and seasonings your recipe calls for, and toss before moving them to the tray. Mixing in a bowl gives you a chance to spread the seasoning around instead of leaving it on one corner. When the vegetables are cooked, taste a piece before adding any finishing salt.

A pot of soup

Check your stock and other ingredients first: they may already contain seasoning. Add salt gradually, stir, and check the taste near the end of cooking. Keep a short note of the amount that worked with that particular stock if this is a meal you make regularly.

A simple salad dressing

Combine the dressing ingredients in a small bowl or jar, mix well, and taste it with a piece of the salad rather than only from the spoon. The dressing and the finished salad can taste different. Start small and adjust the complete dish.

Keep the salt dry after opening

A convenient spot beside a steaming pan is not necessarily a good storage spot. Use a clean, tightly closed food container and keep it in a dry cupboard away from cooking steam. Use a dry spoon when taking salt out, and close the container after each use. These habits are consistent with Morton's storage guidance, which recommends cool, dry storage and an airtight container where needed.

Keep the original label or a clear photo of it, especially if you transfer the salt into another container. You will still have the product name and any handling instructions when it is time to reorder. Follow the directions on your own package; general storage advice does not replace them.
