Most of us grew up thinking of salt as white — the thing we sprinkle on food. It is also what many American ranchers have set out for their cattle and other livestock. In recent years, farmers, ranchers and dairies have increasingly used pink Himalayan rock salt instead. In Europe, that switch happened over the past few decades; more US and Canadian operations have been moving the same way.

Why the difference matters

Salt is sodium chloride, and every livestock salt is mostly exactly that — whether it is white, pink or grey. Two practical differences matter more than colour: how the salt holds up outdoors, and what the product label states it contains. Buy on the guaranteed analysis and the price per pound, not on the colour of the block.

Rock salt holds up in weather

Manufactured flake and loose salts can dissolve or cake after rain. Rock salt blocks are denser, so they weather more slowly, are less likely to break up in a cow's mouth, and generally last longer in the pasture — which is a real cost difference across a season.

What pink salt is, and what it is not

Himalayan pink rock salt is mined rock salt: sodium chloride with naturally occurring trace amounts of other minerals, which is where the pink colour comes from. Trace amounts are not a mineral program. Salt does not supply copper, zinc, manganese, selenium, iodine or cobalt in the balanced amounts a herd needs, and it is not a treatment for any condition or a way to improve milk production, growth or fertility on its own.

Livestock need two things, offered separately and free-choice: salt, and a mineral supplement chosen for your forage and your veterinarian's or nutritionist's advice. A forage test is the only reliable way to know which minerals your herd is short of.
